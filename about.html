About this site

A hundred and thirty years of unanswered arithmetic

What the numbers are, where they come from, and how this site separates what is documented from what is being proposed.

What this site is

In 1891 Hazrat Mirza Ghulam Ahmad of Qadian recorded a revelation that took an unusual form. Instead of words, it arrived as sixty-five numbers arranged in five rows, with a small hand-drawn symbol beside them and a line of Arabic beneath.

The numbers have never been convincingly explained. In June 2022 the Review of Religions published an open appeal for codebreakers, setting out the history of failed attempts — Abjad letter-values, binary encodings, Qur'anic verse indices, coordinates, Fibonacci relations.

This site presents one further attempt: that the numbers are atomic numbers, and that the sequence maps onto stellar nucleosynthesis — the process by which stars manufacture the elements. It sets the proposal out in full, including the places where it does not work.

Where the numbers come from

The revelation was published in Ilham and later reproduced in The Heavenly Decree, issued by Islam International Publications. The figures are written in Urdu-style numerals and read right to left in the original; the transliteration used throughout this site follows the ordering given in the published sources.

A note recorded alongside them observed that their meaning was known to God alone, and would be made plain at the appropriate time. The Arabic inscription reads:

Peace be upon the one who fathoms our mysteries and follows the guidance.

Hazrat Mirza Ghulam Ahmad

Born in Qadian, Punjab, in 1835, Mirza Ghulam Ahmad founded the Ahmadiyya Muslim Community in 1889 and declared himself the Promised Messiah and Mahdi awaited in Islamic tradition. He wrote prolifically in Urdu, Arabic and Persian across some eighty works, and died in 1908.

The community he founded is now established in more than two hundred countries and is known for its motto, Love for All, Hatred for None. Its claims regarding his status are a matter of religious conviction, and are not the subject of this site.

How to read what follows

Three different kinds of statement appear on this site, and it is worth keeping them apart:

Documented. The sixty-five numbers themselves, their arrangement, the date, the publication history. These are matters of record and can be checked against the sources.

Established science. The account of stellar nucleosynthesis — the burning ladder, the nickel-56 decay chain, the r-process. None of this is speculative. It rests on B²FH (1957) and six decades of work since, and is checkable against any astrophysics textbook.

Proposed. The mapping between the two. This is an interpretation, not a result. It is offered for examination rather than acceptance, and the section titled What this reading does not explain sets out the strongest objections to it — including the absence of carbon and oxygen, which is a real problem and not a small one.

Editorial approach

On the difference between a finding and a claim

Arguments of this shape — a scripture or revelation appearing to anticipate a modern scientific discovery — deserve care, because they are easy to make badly. Patterns are cheap. Given enough numbers and a rich enough body of science, something will always line up.

The approach taken here is to state the pattern precisely, show the arithmetic so anyone can verify it, and then argue against it as honestly as possible. Every count on this site has been checked directly against the published sequence rather than repeated from secondary sources. Where an earlier version of this argument contained errors, they have been corrected rather than quietly carried forward.

What remains is genuinely curious. Whether it is more than curious is exactly the question, and it is not one this site claims to have closed.

If you can strengthen the argument, or take it apart, both are welcome. The discussion page is open, and corrections can be sent directly.
